# Domain Research Prompt: AI for Designers
## Run in Claude, GPT-4o, and Gemini — combine outputs

I am designing a practitioner handbook called "AI for Designers:
A Practitioner's Guide" as part of an AI+1 curriculum — designers
(graphic designers, brand designers, UX/UI designers, illustrators,
art directors, motion designers, and independent creative directors)
who keep their professional identity and add AI fluency on top,
rather than becoming generic prompt engineers.

Research the following:

---

**1. Current AI tool adoption in design by professional role**

Map AI tool adoption across distinct design roles: graphic designers,
brand identity designers, UX/UI designers, illustrators, art directors,
motion designers, and independent creative directors working with
one primary client relationship. Which tools are actually being used,
not just marketed? Name specific platforms (Midjourney, Adobe Firefly,
Figma AI, Canva AI, DALL-E 3, Stable Diffusion, Runway, Pika, Adobe
Sensei, Framer AI, Galileo AI, Uizard, Khroma, Looka, etc.) with
evidence of adoption rates and what functions they perform. Distinguish
between tools that automate Tier 1 work (pattern execution, asset
generation, draft creation, resizing, color palette generation) and
tools being sold as judgment replacements (brand strategy, creative
direction, client relationship management).

---

**2. AI failure modes specific to design — documented cases and
structural reasons**

Where does AI get design wrong in ways that create professional,
financial, or reputational exposure for the designer?

- Brand voice and identity drift: what do AI generation tools miss
  about brand consistency, and what are the consequences when a client
  receives assets that are technically correct but brand-wrong?

- Client relationship misread: how does AI fail to understand the
  unspoken brief — the things a client wants but cannot articulate —
  that an experienced designer reads from years of relationship context?

- Taste calibration failures: what does AI generate when asked to
  produce work "in the style of" a specific client's brand, and where
  does it systematically fail the aesthetic judgment a senior designer
  applies?

- Copyright and IP exposure: what are the documented legal risks of
  using AI-generated assets in client deliverables — training data
  provenance, style copying, image licensing? Name specific cases
  or rulings.

- Presentation and rationale failure: what does AI miss when a
  designer presents work to a client — the narrative, the reasoning,
  the ability to defend a creative decision under pressure?

- Revision cycle failures: where does AI-assisted design break down
  in the back-and-forth of client feedback and iteration?

---

**3. The copyright and IP landscape for AI-generated design assets**

What is the current legal status of AI-generated images in the US
and EU for commercial use?

- What have the major copyright rulings (Thaler v. Vidal, Getty
  Images v. Stability AI, class action suits against Midjourney,
  Stability AI, DeviantArt) established or left unresolved?

- What does the US Copyright Office's current guidance say about
  AI-assisted works vs. AI-generated works?

- What are the practical implications for a designer delivering
  AI-assisted assets to a client? What disclosures are prudent?
  What contract language is emerging?

- Which AI image generation platforms have the cleanest IP
  provenance (trained on licensed data) vs. the highest litigation
  risk?

- What does a designer need to know before using any AI-generated
  asset in a client deliverable?

---

**4. The fluency trap in design — where AI output looks professional
but isn't**

The fluency trap is AI output that looks like professional design
work to a non-designer but would not survive expert review.

- What are the most common fluency trap patterns in AI-generated
  design: technically correct but typographically weak, visually
  coherent but brand-incoherent, aesthetically polished but
  conceptually empty?

- How do experienced designers describe the difference between
  AI-generated work and human design work? What do they see that
  non-designers miss?

- Where is the fluency trap most dangerous — in client presentations,
  in portfolio building, in spec work, in design education?

- What happens when a designer uses AI to generate work they cannot
  explain, defend, or iterate on when the client pushes back?

---

**5. The labor market impact on designers**

- Which design job functions are experiencing the most disruption
  from AI? Which are seeing wage premiums for AI fluency?

- How is AI affecting the junior/mid-level designer pipeline at
  agencies, in-house teams, and freelance markets?

- What skills are design schools (RISD, Parsons, ArtCenter, SVA),
  professional organizations (AIGA, IDSA, IxDA), and major employers
  (IDEO, Pentagram, AKQA, in-house teams at Apple, Google, Airbnb)
  emphasizing in response to AI?

- Is there data on AI wage premiums in design analogous to the
  PwC 56% cross-industry finding? What does the design-specific
  data show?

- How are the major platforms (Adobe, Figma, Canva) positioning
  AI in their product strategy and what does that mean for
  designers who depend on those platforms?

- What is the net employment trajectory for designers: which
  specializations are growing, which are declining, and what
  does the transition period look like for a mid-career designer?

---

**6. The irreducibly human taxonomy for designers**

What does an experienced designer know that no current AI system
can replicate?

- Client intuition: how do experienced designers read what a
  client actually wants vs. what they say they want, and how
  does that reading develop over a long-term relationship?

- Taste and aesthetic judgment: what is taste in design, how
  is it developed, and why is it not a pattern-matching problem
  that AI can solve?

- Creative accountability: what does it mean for a designer to
  own a creative decision — to stake their professional reputation
  on a direction — and why does that accountability matter to
  clients?

- Constraint navigation: how do experienced designers work within
  business, technical, and cultural constraints in ways that
  produce better work, not just constrained work?

- Brief interpretation: what happens in the gap between what a
  client writes in a brief and what the work should actually do,
  and who closes that gap?

- Presentation and persuasion: why is the ability to present,
  defend, and iterate on creative work in a client meeting
  a core design competency, not a soft skill?

- Cultural and contextual reading: what does a designer need
  to understand about culture, context, and audience that
  AI systems trained on historical data systematically miss?

**7. Existing AI training and education for designers**

What training programs exist for designers seeking AI fluency?

- Adobe, Figma, and Canva's own AI training resources

- AIGA, IDSA, IxDA courses or guidance on AI in design practice

- Design school programs incorporating AI (RISD, Parsons, SVA,
  ArtCenter, RCA)

- Independent educators, YouTube channels, and community resources
  (e.g., The Futur, Flux Academy, CharliMarieTV) covering AI
  for designers

- What do these programs teach, and what do they miss?

- Is there a course or resource specifically designed for AI+1
  in design — preserving designer identity while adding AI fluency,
  rather than training designers to become prompt engineers?

---

**8. The one-client freelance designer context**

The AI+1 designer handbook is specifically designed for freelance
designers with one primary client relationship — not agency designers
working on multiple accounts, not in-house designers embedded in a
product team.

- What is the scale of this market? How many designers work primarily
  in a one-client or few-client freelance model?

- What are the specific AI risks for this model that differ from
  agency or in-house contexts? (Client confidentiality, brand
  exclusivity, relationship dependency, no institutional backup
  if an AI-assisted deliverable fails.)

- What does AI change about the economics of one-client freelance
  design? Does it compress rates, enable expansion, or both?

- What does a one-client freelance designer need to know about
  AI that an agency designer does not?

**Flag:** Any evidence of the fluency trap in design — where
AI output looks like professional design work but lacks the
craft judgment, client knowledge, and creative accountability
that makes it defensible. Specific attention to: AI-generated
brand identity systems presented to clients as complete,
AI-assisted logo design delivered without rationale, AI image
assets in client deliverables with unresolved IP provenance,
and AI-generated portfolio pieces that misrepresent a
designer's actual capabilities.
