## Figure 1.1 — The fluency trap as a two-component mechanism (Critical, MC)
**Trigger:** "It runs on two independent components — statistical coherence without communicative intent (the Stochastic Parrots finding) and the cognitive reward for processing fluency (the Alter-Oppenheimer finding) — and the two components amplify each other. The output looks right; the reader's brain confirms it feels right; nobody checks whether it is informed."
**Figure type:** systems diagram
**SCOPE**
- S: single-column 89mm, 300 DPI, flat vector
- C: 5 confirmed elements — (1) Model output node (statistical coherence, no communicative intent); (2) Reader cognition node (processing fluency reward); (3) the amplification link joining the two (mutual reinforcement loop between 1 and 2); (4) the "informed?" check node that is bypassed; (5) the closed-trap terminal state. Optional 6th: the professional-judgment interruption point shown as a blockage (⊣) on the link into node 5.
- O: left-to-right causal chain — node 1 → node 2, with a curved feedback arc between them (amplification); both feed forward past the bypassed check node (shown off the main line, with a broken/dashed connector to mark it as skipped) → trap-closed terminal. The judgment-interruption blockage symbol (⊣) sits on the final segment to show where the chain can be broken.
- P: flat vector, Okabe-Ito palette — node 1 Blue #0072B2, node 2 Orange #E69F00, amplification arc Vermillion #D55E00, bypassed check node Black #000000 outline only (hollow), terminal trap state Reddish Purple #CC79A7, interruption blockage Bluish Green #009E73; uniform 1pt strokes, white background, unannotated (no baked-in text)
- E: do NOT show the creative-brief example, do NOT show the designer/client, do NOT include the academic citations or author names, do NOT depict the AI+1 frame or the human-competency taxonomy — this figure is the mechanism only
