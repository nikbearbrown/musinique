### SECTION 3: Banks
**Core Claim:** High street banks offer a range of lending products to small businesses and are "open for business," with 80%+ approval rates.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- BBA data: 117,000 SME loan/overdraft applications in Q3 2012, approval above 80% for smaller businesses, £6.2bn new facilities approved
- Individual bank profiles: Barclays, RBS/NatWest, Lloyds, Santander, Metro, HSBC — each with specific free banking periods and product listings
- Darren Fenton case study: £15,000 overdraft secured from Ulster Bank after First Trust refused

**Logical Method:** The BBA interview functions as institutional endorsement. Individual bank profiles function as product listings. The Fenton case study illustrates persistence and shopping around as effective strategies.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The BBA interview is structurally compromised as evidence: Irene Graham represents the banks' trade association. She is explaining her members' positions, not independently assessing lending conditions. The claim that "if your business proposition is viable, the banks are very much open for business" defines viability in circular terms — banks are lending to businesses they define as viable, and the businesses that fail to get loans are, by definition, not viable. This is unfalsifiable.
- The 80% approval rate requires context the guide does not provide: What proportion of eligible businesses applied? What was the rejection experience for genuinely early-stage startups without trading history or collateral — the audience this guide most directly addresses?
- Tracy Gray case study (Button and Bean, £5,000 Santander loan): "From pulling together the information to securing the loan took one year." A one-year timeline to secure a £5,000 loan is a significant obstacle to growth, presented without commentary on whether this is typical or exceptional.

**Methodological Soundness:** The bank section functions as a bank directory with institutional PR layered over it. The BBA interview is advocacy, not analysis. The case studies are honest about the process (one year for £5,000 is not flattering) but are presented as success stories rather than data points in a distribution of outcomes.

## Extended Research Notes

**Pantry note:** `pantry/notes-banks.md`

Key additions: bank lending is organized around repayment capacity, collateral, credit history, cash flow, and covenant risk. Approval statistics are conditional on who applies and who is discouraged from applying. Trade-association claims should be treated as stakeholder evidence rather than neutral evidence.

Settled: banks are conservative lenders, and early-stage firms often face credit constraints because they lack track record or collateral. Contested: whether approval-rate data primarily measures lender restrictiveness, borrower self-selection, or the health of applicant firms.

Teaching move: diagram the difference between all firms needing capital, firms eligible for bank debt, firms that apply, and firms that are approved.
