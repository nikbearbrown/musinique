### CHAPTERS 3–5: The Ascending Spiral of Abstraction — Classical Physics and the Birth of the Blind Spot
**Core Claim:** Classical physics from Galileo to Hamilton represents a genuine achievement — an ascending spiral of mathematical abstraction that produced extraordinary predictive power — but this very success made it easy to mistake the map for the territory. Newton's absolute time, the Lagrangian, and Hamiltonian phase space are instruments for knowing how things behave; they are not descriptions of how things are in themselves.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Detailed historical reconstruction: Mertonians' development of kinematics, Galileo's workshop method, Descartes' analytic geometry, Newton's laws, Lagrange's generalized coordinates, Hamilton's phase space
- Newton's theological grounding: absolute space and time as attributes of God, the "view from nowhere" that became the implicit epistemological ideal of classical physics
- The principle of least action as an example of abstraction that nonetheless has its roots in embodied experience of economy of effort
- Boltzmann's H-theorem and the arrow of time: time's direction emerges from coarse-graining, which itself depends on the finite precision of real observers. Time's arrow is therefore irreducibly experiential

**Logical Method:** Intellectual history combined with philosophical interpretation. The authors show that the very success of classical physics created conditions for its misinterpretation. The ascending spiral of abstraction is reconstructed not to dismiss it but to show that each step in the spiral depended on and retained connections to embodied experience that the blind spot worldview later severed.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The treatment of the arrow of time via Boltzmann is the most important and most contested section of these chapters. The argument that coarse-graining (which depends on the finite precision of actual observers) is responsible for time's arrow is one plausible view but is not the consensus position. The authors appropriately acknowledge this is contested but do not engage the strongest competing accounts (e.g., the past hypothesis, gravitational entropy).
- The "Loschmidt paradox" resolution offered — that the apparent reversibility of microscopic mechanics does not undermine temporal asymmetry because observers cannot measure with infinite precision — is stated but not defended against the objection that the real problem is the low-entropy initial condition of the universe, not measurement limitations.

**Methodological Soundness:** Strong as intellectual history. Somewhat weaker as philosophy of physics, where the authors make tendentious moves that specialists would contest without sufficient acknowledgment.
