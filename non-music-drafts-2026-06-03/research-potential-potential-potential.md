### SECTION 2: Potential, Potential, Potential (Chapter 2 — Hammerbacher Biography)
**Core Claim:** Jeffrey Hammerbacher's career trajectory—from factory worker's son in Indiana to quant at Bear Stearns to data team founder at Facebook to medical AI researcher at Mount Sinai—illustrates both the social mobility of the data economy and the substantive argument that data science should be directed at problems of genuine human importance rather than ad optimization.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Hammerbacher coined "data scientist" at Facebook (2006–2008), co-founded Cloudera; Cloudera valued at $4.1 billion in April 2014 on Intel investment
- Hammerbacher's own critique: "The best minds of my generation are thinking about how to make people click ads. That sucks." (Bloomberg Businessweek, 2011)
- His reading list exceeds 1,100 books; described by former colleague Adam DeAngelo as unusually extroverted for someone with deep technical skill; described by Rothschild as entrepreneurially self-confident
- Hammerbacker's educational trajectory: failed all Harvard sophomore finals, was fired from two jobs, returned to Harvard after a year of factory and retail work—what the book frames as a "turning point"

**Logical Method:** Full biographical narrative—an extended character study used to personify and humanize the abstract concept of the data-first mindset. Hammerbacher is the book's representative individual.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The biography is engaging but its argumentative function is muddled. Is Hammerbacker evidence that the data economy is a meritocracy? That exceptional people drive technological eras? That directing data science toward medicine is better than ad targeting? The chapter implies all three without establishing any rigorously.
- Survivorship bias is unacknowledged. Hammerbacker's unconventional path worked out spectacularly. The chapter provides no evidence about the typical outcomes of similar profiles—brilliant, non-conformist, institutionally resistant data people who did not happen to arrive at Facebook in 2006 and did not have Rothschild introduce them to VCs.
- His critique of consumer internet ad targeting is presented sympathetically but without the counterargument Lohr later makes (printing press → religious tracts → democratization of knowledge). Both arguments are in the book, but in different sections without direct engagement.

**Methodological Soundness:** A profile piece, not an argument. The rhetorical function is character-building for the book's human throughline. The logical contribution to the central thesis is indirect.
