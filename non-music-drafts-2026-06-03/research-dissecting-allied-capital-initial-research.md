### SECTION 4: Dissecting Allied Capital — Initial Research (Chapter 5)
**Core Claim:** Allied Capital, a Business Development Company, systematically violated SEC fair value accounting requirements by substituting an SBA-style "permanent impairment" standard, enabling it to conceal portfolio deterioration, recognize non-cash income from related-party controlled subsidiaries, and repeatedly raise equity from retail investors while the underlying portfolio deteriorated.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Database construction: every Allied loan tracked at cost and fair value quarterly over several years, mirroring the Siram methodology
- Pattern confirmed: equity kickers written down while related loans held at cost — a statistically reliable predictor of future loan writedowns
- Two extended calls with Allied Investor Relations (Suzanne Sparrow) and CFO Penny Roll documented (May 2002): Roll explicitly described a permanent-impairment standard for writedowns; explicitly stated grade-four loans would be held at cost; compared Allied's performance favorably to "much safer senior bank loans"
- Allied's stated credit loss rate: less than 1% per year on mezzanine loans — described as "absurd" given that excellent senior bank loan portfolios typically generate 3–4% loss rates
- Specific securities: Velocita bonds carried at $4M while trading at 2 cents on dollar; StarTech DIP loan described as "money good" despite monthly revenues having fallen to $5M; NetTel in Chapter 7 liquidation for over a year while Allied described "asset sales which are imminent"
- BLX: 14.5% delinquency rate in securitization prospectus; 25% interest rate charged by Allied to its own subsidiary
- Arthur Anderson removal of specific audit language (identical pattern to Siram in 1997)

**Logical Method:** Statistical pattern analysis + documentary evidence + direct management admission.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The initial position size (7.5% of fund at $26.25) is stated before the thesis is fully developed. The book's retrospective structure makes the research appear more complete at the time of investment than it may have been.
- Einhorn correctly identifies that Roll's description of their valuation methodology ("permanent impairment") violates SEC requirements, but the book acknowledges he could not fully assess the magnitude of the overvaluation at this stage.

**Methodological Soundness:** Strongest section of the book. The combination of documented management admissions, statistical loan pattern analysis, and specific securities with objectively verifiable market prices creates a multi-method convergent case. The methodology described is explicitly what the SEC would later confirm.
