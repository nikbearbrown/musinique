### CHAPTER 3: The Migratory Soul — Where Self Lives

**Core Claim:** The concept of "self" has migrated historically from the heart/chest/whole body to the brain, and within the brain increasingly to the left hemisphere and to rational/analytical function. This migration parallels and reinforces the design of the digital computer as an idealized rational agent. Both migrations are distortions.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Egyptian mummification preserved all organs except the brain (considered useless), while the heart was kept in the body as the center of personhood
- Ancient Greek etymology: psyche evolved from a whole-body animating force to an intellectualized capacity for reason (Plato, Aristotle, Stoics)
- Descartes located the soul in the pineal gland; eliminated souls from non-human animals entirely
- Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics*: eudaimonia requires arete, fulfillment of one's purpose; Aristotle identified contemplation (reason) as that purpose—conveniently for a philosopher
- Split-brain patients (Gazzaniga's work): the left hemisphere "interpreter" confabulates explanations for actions generated by the right hemisphere; the self is a story the left tells itself
- Oliver Sacks: neurological history has been a history of the left hemisphere; right hemisphere capacities were "contemptuously" regarded as primitive
- Claude Shannon's 1937 master's thesis: Boolean logic can be implemented electrically, founding digital computing on Aristotelian logic
- Dave Ackley (UNM/Santa Fe Institute): computers inherit an "IOU for a body"—designed as disembodied rational agents on the Turing machine model
- Statistical/neural approaches to language, translation, and object recognition now outperform rule-based algorithmic approaches—the messy, right-hemisphere methods are winning

**Logical Method:** Historical sweep → philosophical synthesis → contemporary AI implications. Christian traces the Western bias toward reason from ancient Greece through Descartes through Von Neumann architecture, then shows the contemporary reversal: statistical, embodied, messy computation now dominates.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The claim that left-hemisphere dominance in culture *caused* or *reinforced* the design of digital computers involves significant inferential leaps. Turing's Turing machine models a *human computer* doing arithmetic—it was descriptive, not normative
- The neuroscience claims (Sacks, Gazzaniga, Ramachandran) are real but the argument requires that educational and cultural left-hemisphere bias have measurable downstream effects on AI design—this is more associative than causal
- Ken Robinson's education critique is cited as evidence but represents advocacy, not data

**Methodological Soundness:** The strongest sections are the split-brain research (well-documented) and the historical etymology of "soul" (carefully hedged). The grand synthesis of Greek philosophy → Cartesian rationalism → computer architecture → cultural pathology is intellectually suggestive but involves aggregated inferences at each step.
