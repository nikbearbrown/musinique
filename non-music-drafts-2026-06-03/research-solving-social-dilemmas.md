### CHAPTER 8: Solving Social Dilemmas
**Core Claim:** Social tools reduce the prisoners' dilemma through the "shadow of the future," but they also enable the formation of groups society disapproves of — the pro-ana movement being the central case. Falling transaction costs benefit all groups, not just approved ones.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Axelrod's TIT FOR TAT: the iterated prisoners' dilemma shows that repeated interaction enables cooperation because of the shadow of the future
- Putnam's *Bowling Alone*: declining social capital documented across decades of indicators from picnicking to league bowling
- Meetup's most popular early groups: witches, pagans, X Jehovah's Witnesses, Ultima players — not the rotary clubs and bowling leagues Heiferman anticipated. The groups most disadvantaged by prior coordination costs benefited most
- Pro-ana groups: sharing tips to maintain anorexia, including dangerous physical techniques for suppressing hunger; YM shut down its health bulletin board rather than police it
- HSBC, airlines: previously atomized consumers coordinating to exert pressure on large institutions

**Logical Method:** Social capital theory (Putnam, Axelrod) → empirical cases showing social tools building new social capital → acknowledgment of the symmetric problem (tools work for disapproved groups too) → the "publish then filter" structural conclusion.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The pro-ana case is the book's most honest confrontation with its own thesis, but Shirky resolves it too quickly. His conclusion — that "falling transaction costs benefit all groups, not just groups we happen to approve of" — is accurate but insufficient as a response to a crisis where teenage girls are sharing techniques for life-threatening self-harm. The structural observation does not discharge the ethical question.
- The Meetup analysis assumes that latent groups forming = social good. The evidence shows that previously-disadvantaged groups (pagans, X Jehovah's Witnesses) benefited. But the same logic applies to groups whose latency was enforced for protective reasons (pro-ana networks, as Shirky himself acknowledges). The asymmetry between these cases is not resolved.
- Putnam's *Bowling Alone* is used to establish that social capital has been declining — but Shirky does not engage with Putnam's specific causal arguments (television, suburbanization, generational turnover) or assess whether digital social capital substitutes for or merely supplements the civic forms Putnam documented.

**Methodological Soundness:** The symmetric harm acknowledgment is the book's most intellectually honest passage. The resolution — "society will have to shift from preventing groups from forming to actively deciding which to oppose" — is accurate but undersells the difficulty of that transition.
