### CHAPTER 8: Science — Collaboration, Competition, and Reputation

**Core Claim:** Science is an exemplary collective enterprise: organized as a decentralized gift economy with a functioning aggregation mechanism (peer review and citation), it achieves collective truth-finding through the self-interested behavior of competitors. The SARS network of 2003 is the chapter's central case.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- The SARS discovery (March–April 2003): WHO assembled 11 labs across 9 countries; daily teleconferences, shared electron microscope images and virus samples; coronavirus identified as the cause within one month of collaboration beginning
- Henry Oldenburg (1665): founder of *Philosophical Transactions*, pioneer of open scientific publication; established the norm that scientists give up sole ownership of ideas in exchange for public credit
- DeSolla Price and Beaver (1966): the most prolific scientists were also the most collaborative
- Harriet Zuckerman: Nobel laureates collaborated more frequently than comparable scientists
- The Matthew Effect (Merton): famous scientists get disproportionate credit; Lewontin-Hubby study: paper listed under Lewontin's name was cited 50% more than the same paper listed under Hubby's name despite being genuinely equal contributions

**Logical Method:** Historical case study, citation analysis, sociological theory.

**Logical Gaps:**
- Surowiecki acknowledges the Matthew Effect directly but treats it as an interesting anomaly rather than a challenge to his thesis. If the most-cited scientists are not the most correct but merely the most prominent, then the aggregation mechanism (citation/credit) is systematically biased, and collective wisdom may be accreting around the wrong nodes.
- The SARS collaboration succeeded under extreme urgency and with WHO institutional coordination. Surowiecki implies this is a model for ordinary science, but the SARS network was explicitly assembled by an authority (WHO), had a clear problem definition, had built-in resolution criteria (can we confirm coronavirus causes SARS?), and operated under crisis conditions. These are not typical conditions for normal science.
- The "gift economy" framing of science is presented as a durable norm, but Surowiecki acknowledges it is eroding under commercialization. He does not assess how much it has already eroded.

**Methodological Soundness:** Strong descriptive account of collaborative science. The normative claims about the gift economy are more vulnerable than the analysis acknowledges.
