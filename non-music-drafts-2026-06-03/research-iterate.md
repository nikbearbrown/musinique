### SECTION 6: Chapter 5 — Iterate
**Core Claim:** Capital raising requires ongoing market feedback, willingness to niche down further, and speed of implementation to exploit what is working and abandon what isn't.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Wilson's own niche iteration: "family office" → "single family office" → "how to start a family office" → "endowment funds"
- Domain acquisitions as evidence of iteration: FamilyOffices.com, SingleFamilyOffices.com, Endowments.com, MasterLimitedPartnerships.com
- Speed of implementation anecdote: wrote a book on an airplane overnight after being challenged by a banker
- Unique Ability concept from Dan Sullivan's Strategic Coach program

**Logical Method:** Personal case study as primary evidence, supported by practitioner conceptual frameworks (Sullivan's Unique Ability, Jay Abraham's "business within your business").

**Logical Gaps:**
- The chapter's primary evidence for the iterate-and-niche-down strategy is Wilson's own business evolution over more than a decade. This is a single-firm case study and selection-survivor by definition. Firms that iterated and failed are not represented.
- "Find the Business Within Your Business" (Jay Abraham concept) and "Unique Ability" (Dan Sullivan) are presented as received wisdom from recognized experts without any evaluation of their evidence base. Sullivan's Unique Ability framework is a coaching heuristic, not a validated framework. Jay Abraham's $9.4B in sales figure is his own marketing claim and cannot be independently verified. Both are presented as if they are empirical findings.
- The speed of implementation example (writing a book on an airplane overnight) is presented as evidence that content production can be done rapidly. A book written in one transatlantic flight is almost certainly not a book by the standards of the investor funnel the author has spent three chapters constructing. Wilson himself acknowledges elsewhere that "80% of the content should come from you" and warns against poor-quality ghostwriting. The overnight book anecdote contradicts the quality discipline the book elsewhere espouses.
- "Can I Hire Someone to Raise Capital?" — this section correctly identifies the limits of outsourcing the inbound authority strategy ("only you can wave your flag"). But it does not acknowledge the structural reality that most institutional capital raising at scale does involve specialized third-party marketers, placement agents, and distribution professionals. Wilson himself began his career as a third-party marketer. The section's sweeping claim that "you can't rely on someone else to get it done for you" is inconsistent with his own career origin and with standard industry practice for funds above a certain AUM threshold.

**Methodological Soundness:** The iterate chapter is the book's thinnest evidentiary section. It functions primarily as narrative — Wilson's business evolution story — presented as generalizable strategy. The underlying principle (adapt based on market feedback rather than persisting with a failed approach) is sound and underemphasized in most capital raising advice. But the chapter treats anecdote as proof in ways that the earlier chapters, at their best, avoided.
