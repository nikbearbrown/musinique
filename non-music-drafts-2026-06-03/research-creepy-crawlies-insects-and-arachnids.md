### CHAPTER 6: Creepy Crawlies — Insects and Arachnids

**Core Claim:** Insects and arachnids are the numerically dominant, evolutionarily most successful, and ecologically most essential animal group on Earth; their ongoing population collapse represents one of the most consequential environmental crises of the present era.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- China's Four Pests Campaign (1958–1961): elimination of ~1 billion sparrows led to locust explosion, crop failure, and famine; Chinese government acknowledges ≥15 million deaths; historians estimate up to 45 million
- Estimated 10 quintillion (10¹⁹) land arthropods; >half of 2.1 million named animal species are insects and arachnids; ~500 million billion beetles in 380,000 named species
- Insects evolved flight 406 million years ago — at least 150 million years before pterosaurs
- Insect complete metamorphosis allows larvae and adults to occupy different ecological niches, reducing intraspecies competition
- Leaf-cutter ant colonies: cover 50 m², reach 8 m deep, up to 8,000 chambers, 3 million individuals; 29-step division of labor; farm *Leucoagaricus gongylophorus* fungus; maintain bacteria that secrete growth hormones and antifungals — four-way symbiosis running for 50 million years
- 2017 Radboud University (Netherlands): insect biomass in German nature reserves fell 75% over 26 years (1989–2014)
- 2019 global review (73 reports): 41% of insect species declining; one-third endangered; extinction rate 8x faster than mammals/birds/reptiles; total insect mass falling 2.5% annually; Puerto Rico rainforest: 98% decline in ground insects over 35 years
- UK: butterfly species fell 58% on farmed land 2000–2009; 23 bee/wasp species extinct since 1850; monarch butterflies down 80% in 10 years pre-2016; swift numbers down 66% in past decade

**Logical Method:** Historical cautionary tale (Four Pests Campaign as proof of systemic ecological consequences) → scale of arthropod dominance → evolutionary success mechanisms (flight, metamorphosis, social organization) → collapse evidence → call for revised relationship.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The Four Pests famine attribution is presented as established. The text acknowledges "causes were more complicated than simply the failure of the crops," then immediately returns to the sparrow-locust chain as the primary mechanism. Mao's agricultural collectivization policies (the Great Leap Forward) are the primary cause identified by most historians, with the sparrow campaign as a contributing but not primary factor. The framing understates this.
- The 75% insect biomass decline (Hallmann et al. 2017) is a significant finding from German nature reserves using malaise traps. The text presents it as global evidence, but the study was Germany-specific and methodological questions about trap placement and seasonal variation have been raised. The 2019 global review (Sánchez-Bayo & Wyckhuys) is also cited — but that meta-analysis has been criticized for publication bias in the studies it included.
- The claim that bees, wasps, and beetles "pollinate three-quarters of our food crops" requires qualification: the proportion varies significantly by caloric contribution vs. species count. About 75% of flowering plant *species* depend on animal pollinators, but the majority of human *calories* (wheat, rice, maize, soy) are wind-pollinated.

**Methodological Soundness:** The chapter's core empirical claim — that insect populations are declining significantly — is supported by multiple independent lines of evidence. The precise figures for individual countries and species are well-sourced. The evolutionary chronology (flight emergence, Carboniferous megafauna) is consistent with the fossil record.

## Extended Research Notes

**Pantry note:** `pantry/notes-creepy-crawlies-insects-and-arachnids.md`

Key additions: Keep the decline argument strong by keeping denominators visible: biomass versus species richness, Germany versus global evidence, crop species versus human calories, and ecological contribution versus monocausal famine explanation.

Settled: Hallmann et al. 2017 is a major finding on flying-insect biomass decline in German protected areas. Contested: exact global rates and the strength of some apocalypse-style extrapolations.

Teaching move: Ask students to rewrite a headline claim into metric, geography, taxon, time period, and sampling method.
