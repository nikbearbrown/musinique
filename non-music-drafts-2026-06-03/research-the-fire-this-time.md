### INTRODUCTION: The Fire This Time
**Core Claim:** Modern science's worldview is in crisis because it has systematically suppressed the role of direct experience in generating knowledge — the "blind spot" — and this suppression is implicated in civilization-scale existential threats, especially climate change.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- COVID-19 pandemic cited as demonstration of civilization's fragility within the natural world
- The authors name three existing responses to the crisis — scientific triumphalism, science denial, and New Age pseudo-science — and argue all three are dead ends
- The parable of temperature is introduced: the entire edifice of thermodynamics is an abstraction from the concrete bodily experience of hot and cold, which the abstraction cannot replace or fully explain

**Logical Method:** The authors establish crisis by juxtaposition: science is simultaneously our most powerful cognitive tool and the generator of conditions threatening its own civilizational substrate. The blind spot is named via the temperature parable as a concrete illustration of the general structure of the problem.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The link between "blind spot metaphysics" and the specific political failures that produced climate inaction is asserted rather than demonstrated in the Introduction. The causal arrow (blind spot → climate inaction) will need to carry substantial argumentative weight that is not yet provided.
- The temperature parable is vivid but introduces an epistemological claim (direct experience is ineliminable from scientific concepts) as if it were self-evident. The argument requires more rigorous support, which subsequent chapters partially supply.

**Methodological Soundness:** The Introduction functions as a manifesto. It establishes the book's architecture clearly and honestly. Claims are appropriately flagged as arguments-to-be-made rather than established conclusions.
