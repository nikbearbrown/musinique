### CLUSTER F: Checklists — Debt
**Content:** Practical operational guides covering: cash flow and bank lending, bond market structure, financial intermediaries, franchising, credit rating management, receivables as collateral, banking relationships, gearing measurement, money markets, loan agreements, raising capital via bonds, bank financing steps, interest coverage ratios, leverage ratios, debt cover, fixed-charge coverage.

**Logical Assessment:** The checklist cluster is reference material, not analytical content. The definitions are standard and correct. The "Advantages/Disadvantages/Do/Don't" format systematically prevents analytical depth. Several notable specifics:
- The gearing measurement checklist correctly defines D/E, equity ratio, and debt ratio, and correctly notes that "gearing ratios may not always reflect the true nature of a company's accounts" — an honest concession.
- The bond market checklist correctly notes the inverse relationship between interest rates and bond prices.
- The franchise checklist is operationally specific and internally consistent.
- The fixed-charge coverage checklist correctly identifies the ratio formula and its limitations.

**Methodological Soundness:** Adequate as reference. Not designed for analytical use.

## Extended Research Notes

**Pantry note:** `pantry/notes-cluster-f-checklists-debt.md`

Key additions: debt checklists are underwriting prompts, not analysis. DSCR, FCCR, leverage, interest coverage, collateral, and covenants need business-model context and stress-case thinking.

Settled: lenders underwrite repayment capacity and downside protection. Contested: how much ratio-based underwriting misses business quality, owner dependence, and macro shock exposure.

Teaching move: ask what event breaks the ratio and what covenant or collateral protects the lender.
