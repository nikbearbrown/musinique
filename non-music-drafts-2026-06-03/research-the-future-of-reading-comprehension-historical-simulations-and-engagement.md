### SECTION 6: "The Future of Reading Comprehension" — Historical Simulations and Engagement
**Core Claim:** AI can make reading comprehension interactive and immersive through character simulations (Khanmigo as Jay Gatsby, Harriet Tubman, George Washington), enabling engagement that static text cannot produce.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Sanvi (ninth-grade Khan World School student) engagement anecdote: "talked to Jay Gatsby" for reading comprehension
- Gallup/Department of Education (2020): 54% of Americans aged 16–74 read below sixth-grade level
- Washington Post experiment: Gillian Brachelle (Tubman expert) tested the AI Tubman simulation—found it valuable as "hook" despite limitations
- Hallucination example: AI Tubman confirmed attribution of a quote Tubman probably didn't say, rather than flagging the attribution as uncertain

**Logical Method:** Demonstrative anecdote + engagement hypothesis. The case is that simulations increase engagement; increased engagement is assumed to improve comprehension.

**Logical Gaps:**
- Engagement ≠ learning. The Sanvi anecdote demonstrates that a student found the simulation enjoyable and interesting. It does not demonstrate comprehension improvement. This is the ITS literature's most commonly flagged conflation (see AutoTutor bookmap: "engagement ≠ learning" as recurring critique).
- The Tubman hallucination example—AI confirms a likely fabricated quote—is flagged but immediately minimized: "This isn't an outright hallucination." It is, functionally, a misinformation event. The AI told a user a disputed quote was attributable to Tubman. In an educational context, this is precisely the failure mode that most concerns educators.
- "Simulations can bring things alive" is a plausible pedagogical hypothesis. The evidence offered is one student's positive experience and a journalist finding the tool interesting. Neither establishes learning outcomes.

**Methodological Soundness:** Anecdotal. The comprehension improvement hypothesis is plausible but untested by the evidence provided.
