### CHAPTERS 6–7: Selves and Symbols; The Epiphenomenon
**Core Claim:** The I arises from a perceptual loop that inevitably turns back on itself. Symbols in a brain—triggerable structures corresponding to categories—grow to include a symbol for the self, and that self-symbol, becoming self-reinforcing over decades, is what we call an eye. Epi, the non-existent marble in a box of envelopes, models the ontological status of the self: real enough to explain behavior, unreal in any simple material sense.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- The mosquito's minimal symbol repertoire vs. dogs' richer but still limited repertoire vs. adult humans' essentially unlimited extensible repertoire
- The robot vehicles (Stanley) analogy: a vehicle that crosses a desert navigates without anything deserving the name self-awareness
- The Epi box of envelopes: the tactile illusion of a marble created by 100 aligned triple-layered flaps. The marble isn't there but "feels just like a marble"
- The graduated scale from single-cell organisms to humans: self-models require a symbol for "I" which requires perception of self, which requires enough perceptual apparatus

**Logical Method:** Analogical argument (Epi as model for the I's ontological status); inductive argument from the structure of symbol repertoires

**Logical Gaps:**
- The Epi analogy is elegant but may prove too much. The marble in the box is fully explainable as a collective tactile effect of discrete physical parts—there is no mystery about *why* it feels spherical once we know the architecture. But that's exactly what Hofstadter claims about the I: it is fully explainable as a collective effect. The analogy thus undermines the mysteriousness of the I rather than explaining it—which may be his point, but it doesn't explain what *it is like* to be the I
- The mosquito/dog/human graduation is intuitive but the boundary conditions are underspecified. The argument needs to specify what *kind* of category or symbol level is sufficient for self-perception, not just note that humans have more complex ones

**Methodological Soundness:** The Epi model is one of the book's strongest expository moves. The symbol-level argument is coherent but the gap between "a symbol for self exists" and "an I exists" is not fully bridged.
