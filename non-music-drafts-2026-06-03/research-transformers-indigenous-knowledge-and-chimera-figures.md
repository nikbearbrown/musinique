### CHAPTER 4: Transformers — Indigenous Knowledge and Chimera Figures
**Core Claim:** The shamanic concept of transformation—humans becoming jaguars, spirits taking animal form—converges with evolutionary biology's demonstration that all species share genetic material and descend from common ancestors. We are all transformers.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Shawi specialist Na'wiri Rafael Chanchari: stones, trees, and ecosystems all have souls/mothers; a Tana-ashi is a "general soul" of an ecosystem
- Kandoshi specialist Usi Kamarambi: the "owner of animals" appeared as a hybrid being—feathers, human feet, long nails
- Cave art evidence: Lascaux bird-headed man (~17,000 BP), Trois-Frères sorcerer/stag-human (~13,000 BP), Chauvet woman-bison-feline (~31,000 BP)
- Evolutionary biology: 99% of mouse genes found in humans; all species share DNA/RNA machinery

**Logical Method:** Cross-cultural convergence argument: prehistoric imagery + indigenous shamanic cosmology + modern evolutionary genetics all point to human-animal kinship as a deep and ancient recognition.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The interpretation of prehistoric cave paintings as evidence of shamanic practice is contested among archaeologists. The author acknowledges "it is difficult to prove that prehistoric humans practice what we now call shamanism" — but then uses the images as supporting evidence for the convergence thesis anyway. This is acknowledged gap that remains a gap.
- The jump from "all species share genetic heritage" (evolutionary biology) to "shamans are right that humans can transform into jaguars" (shamanic cosmology) is a categorical conflation. Genetic kinship is a structural fact; spiritual transformation is a different kind of claim about a different kind of process. Treating them as evidence for the same thesis confuses metaphor with mechanism.
- The indigenous specialists' framework — animals are human beings in their own world — is reported with respect and without critical engagement. No attempt is made to specify what predictions this framework makes that differ from a secular ecological framework.

**Methodological Soundness:** The archaeological evidence is handled carefully (the author acknowledges its limits). The convergence argument is rhetorically effective but logically imprecise: convergent metaphors in human cultures do not constitute evidence that the metaphors accurately describe biological reality.
