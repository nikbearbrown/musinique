### PREFACE & PROLOGUE
**Core Claim:** The mystery of consciousness—what it is to *be* an I—has preoccupied Hofstadter since adolescence, when confronted with his sister Molly's inability to acquire language. *I Am a Strange Loop* is the long-delayed full articulation of an idea only gestured at in *Gödel, Escher, Bach*: that selves are strange loops.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Personal narrative: discovery of Gödel at 14, early programming, sustained obsession with self-reference and consciousness
- The prologue dialogue (written at 16–17) between pseudo-Plato and pseudo-Socrates already sketches the central problem: that knowing and feeling alive may be reflexive illusions sustained by habit

**Logical Method:** Autobiographical framing as credibility-building; the prologue functions as a "thought experiment fossil"—naive but directionally correct

**Logical Gaps:**
- The preface blurs the distinction between Hofstadter's *personal* conviction that the strange loop idea is true and the *argumentative case* for it. The rhetorical warmth here does work the formal argument must later do independently
- The claim that *GEB*'s "central message went unnoticed" sets up a grievance narrative that shapes how the book is argued—adversarially, against a presumed resistant audience

**Methodological Soundness:** Framing only; no claims yet assessable
