### PROLOGUE / Introduction: The Anti-Fragile

**Core Claim:** There is no word in any language for the opposite of fragile. That opposite — anti-fragility — is the property of systems that gain from disorder, volatility, and stress. Its absence from our vocabulary reflects its absence from our consciousness, and that absence causes enormous, preventable harm.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Survey of Indo-European and Semitic language families confirms no word exists for anti-fragility
- Homer's *wine dark sea* cited as evidence that cultures lack words for things they nonetheless perceive
- The triad (fragile / robust / anti-fragile) is introduced as a map of how all things respond to volatility

**Logical Method:** Definitional argument by negation — the concept is defined by exclusion of its known opposites (fragile and robust), then shown to be a real, distinct property.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The claim that no language has the word is asserted without exhaustive documentation. Taleb acknowledges he checked "most" Indo-European and Semitic families but this is necessarily incomplete.
- The inference from *linguistic absence* to *conceptual blindness* is a reasonable hypothesis but not proven — cultures may grasp a concept without lexicalizing it.

**Methodological Soundness:** Philosophically adequate as a framing device. The definitional work is rigorous: anti-fragility ≠ resilience because resilience returns to baseline; anti-fragility improves beyond it. This distinction is maintained consistently throughout the book.
