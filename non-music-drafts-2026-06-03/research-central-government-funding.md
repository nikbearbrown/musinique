### SECTION 2: Central Government Funding
**Core Claim:** Government offers three routes to small business capital: direct loans and grants, indirect funding through programmes encouraging third-party lending, and public procurement contracts.

**Key Sources Profiled:**

**StartUp Loans**
- Specific, credible: launched May 2012, expanded January 2013 to age 30, total pot £110M over three years, average loan £2,500
- Phil Coombes case study: £7,500 loan after bank rejection due to age and lack of collateral
- Gap: No repayment terms or interest rate stated. A £2,500 average loan is very small capital for most meaningful business activities. The guide doesn't address whether these loans are adequate for their stated purpose.

**New Enterprise Allowance**
- £1,274 over 26 weeks + up to £1,000 loan for start-up costs — specific and sourced to DWP
- Waiting period scrapped as of reporting date
- Gap: Eligibility is restricted to jobseekers. The guide does not address whether a business owner who left employment voluntarily to start a business qualifies.

**Growth Accelerator**
- Notable structure: covers cost of expert engagement rather than providing cash. Business contributes £600 (sub-10 employees) or £1,500 (10–49 employees); government covers the remainder.
- Gap: "Thousands of pounds worth of value" is unquantified. No evaluation data on whether GrowthAccelerator participants outperform non-participants.

**Regional Growth Fund**
- £2.6 billion over 2011–2016, operating through intermediaries not directly to businesses
- Correctly noted as indirect — the guide does not mislead entrepreneurs into believing they can apply directly

**Enterprise Finance Guarantee (EFG)**
- 18,000+ businesses offered loans totalling £1.88 billion since 2009 launch — specific and sourced
- Government guarantees 75% of each loan; borrower pays 2% annual premium to BIS
- Gap: The 2% premium on top of commercial interest rates adds meaningful cost. A business already struggling to meet standard collateral requirements is taking on structurally expensive debt.

**Technology Strategy Board**
- "Over 60% of firms receiving awards are small businesses" — sourced to TSB
- Smart scheme grants cover proof of market, proof of concept, and prototype development — appropriate scope
- Gap: Highly sector-specific (technology, innovation). The guide does not make clear how narrow the eligible universe is.

**Logical Gaps Across Government Section:**
- Government is presented as proactively supportive of small business lending, with BBA's Irene Graham interview reinforcing this framing. The Q&A is with an industry advocate (the banks' own trade association), not an independent assessor of whether banks are actually lending to small businesses at adequate rates. The claim that "more than 80% of 520,000 new lending applications in 2012 were approved" sounds impressive but conceals the denominator problem: how many businesses needed capital but did not apply because they anticipated rejection? The approved/applied ratio is not the same as the funded/needed ratio.

**Methodological Soundness:** The government section is the guide's most factually grounded chapter — specific amounts, schemes, and eligibility criteria. The logical gaps are in framing rather than data: the section presents government as a reliable capital partner without acknowledging that most government schemes are heavily oversubscribed, sector-restricted, or require prior milestones that early-stage businesses cannot demonstrate.
