### SECTION 3: Mackay Preface (1841)
**Core Claim:** The book collects "remarkable instances of those moral epidemics" to demonstrate "how easily the masses had been led astray and how imitative and gregarious men are even in their infatuations and crimes."

**Logical Method:** Explicit statement of thesis as moral taxonomy of collective folly.

**Logical Gaps:**
- "Moral epidemics" is a metaphor that does the analytical work of a mechanism without providing one. Epidemics spread through pathogen transmission with identifiable vectors. Mackay's "epidemics" have no equivalent mechanistic account—they spread by... imitation? desire? proximity? The metaphor forecloses rather than enables analysis.
- The decision to exclude "religious manias" as "incompatible with the limits prescribed" is telling. Financial manias and religious manias share structural features (escalating commitment, social proof, catastrophic collapse). Excluding one class of the phenomenon weakens any claim to general theory.

**Methodological Soundness:** The preface announces an empirical project (collection of instances) but frames it as moral philosophy (demonstration of human gregariousness). These are different goals with different standards of proof.
