### CHAPTER 2: Medium, Message, and Misinformation
**Core Claim:** The internet's structural properties—zero marginal cost of publishing, click-driven economics, algorithmic amplification of outrage—have systematically degraded the information environment by rewarding emotional resonance over accuracy.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Printing press parallel: Philippo de Strata's 1474 complaints mirror contemporary concerns
- BuzzSumo/Steve Rayson study: 100 million articles in 2017; most viral headlines promise emotional experiences ("will make you"), not information
- Facebook: 126 million US users exposed to Russian propaganda (2017 admission)
- MIT study: false news spreads faster and further on Twitter than true news
- Net neutrality comment fraud: 21.7 million comments, ~500,000 sent simultaneously in one second

**Logical Method:** Historical analogy (printing press) + structural economic analysis of click-driven incentives + empirical data on misinformation spread.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The printing press analogy is deployed as comfort ("things turned out fine before") and as warning simultaneously. The authors cannot have it both ways without specifying what conditions made the printing press's disruption ultimately benign and whether those conditions hold today.
- The chapter attributes declining information quality primarily to structural/algorithmic forces but does not adequately weigh demand-side factors: audiences actively prefer emotionally engaging content. This conflation of supply-side and demand-side causes limits prescriptive clarity.
- The Russian propaganda exposure figure (126 million users) is presented without comparison to legitimate political advertising exposure during the same period. The relative magnitude is unknown.

**Methodological Soundness:** The structural analysis is well-grounded. Individual empirical claims are sourced. The chapter's prescriptive conclusion (education is the best defense) is asserted more than derived.
