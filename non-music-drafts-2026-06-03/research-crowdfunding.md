### SECTION 5: Crowdfunding
**Core Claim:** Crowdfunding is the fastest-growing funding category for UK small businesses, with three distinct models (reward, equity, loan) available through multiple platforms.

**Key Platforms Profiled:**

**Crowdcube (equity):** £4.4M raised since February 2011; 64% increase in listing applications 2011–2012. Co-founder advice: be investment ready, engage actively with potential investors.

**Kickstarter (reward):** First UK month: 30 funded projects, £2M raised, £48/minute in pledges. 39% UK backers, 61% international — potentially important for UK businesses.

**Seedrs (equity):** FSA-regulated (only platform with this status at time of writing). Average investment £1,000; individual investments up to £50,000. 7.5% success fee from businesses; investors pay no ongoing fees but share 7.5% of upside with Seedrs.

**Indiegogo (reward/flexible):** 9% fee; 5% refunded if target hit (net 4%); keeps all funds if target missed.

**Sponsume (reward):** 4% fee on successful campaigns; keeps all funds raised regardless of target.

**Buzzbnk (social enterprise):** £25 registration fee; 5% admin on successful campaigns; funds returned if target missed.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Swogo case study (Seedrs): £17,500 raised in 10 days for 5% equity — fastest raise on Seedrs at time of publication. Prior offline networking credited as critical factor.
- Pants to Poverty case study (Buzzbnk): £57,000 "bonk" combining cash return (3%) and product return (7.2% in pants) — hybrid reward/loan structure
- Jeff Lynn (Seedrs) interview: "momentum is everything" — first 10–30% of capital must come from founder's own network before independent investors participate

**Logical Gaps:**
- Platform success rates are conspicuously absent. The guide profiles platforms but provides no data on what percentage of campaigns reach their targets. Kickstarter's own data at the time showed approximately 44% of projects funded — which means 56% of campaigners get nothing and still invest the time and energy of running a campaign. This figure, absent from the guide, is the most important number in the section.
- The Swogo case study is an exceptional outcome (fastest raise in Seedrs history). Using a record-breaking case as primary illustration of the typical crowdfunding experience is survivorship bias in its purest form.
- The Nesta £15 billion projection is repeated from the Introduction without additional scrutiny. No mechanism is offered for how the UK crowdfunding market reaches that figure from its actual 2012 base.
- The FSA regulatory note is important: the regulator's position (crowdfunding investors "will probably lose all their investment if [the business] fails") is quoted accurately. But this appears at the end of the section, after 15+ pages of successful case studies and enthusiastic platform descriptions. The placement systematically minimizes the risk disclosure.

**Methodological Soundness:** The crowdfunding section is well-organized as a platform index. It fails as a risk-adjusted guide to whether crowdfunding is appropriate for a given business. No failure data. No average raise size relative to target. No typical timeline from campaign launch to funded close. The Lynn interview is the most analytically useful content: the network dependency (first 10-30% must come from founder's own network) is a genuine constraint that limits crowdfunding's applicability to businesses without existing audience or community.

## Extended Research Notes

**Pantry note:** `pantry/notes-crowdfunding.md`

Key additions: Separate reward, equity, loan, and donation crowdfunding because each has different success metrics, regulation, and failure modes. A funded campaign is not the same as delivered product, viable business, or positive investor return.

Settled: Platform base rates and risk warnings belong near the front. Kickstarter publishes success statistics; the UK FCA explicitly warns that investment crowdfunding can lead to total loss.

Teaching move: Build a campaign dashboard with launch-to-funded, funded-to-delivered, delivered-to-profitable, and investor-return rates.
