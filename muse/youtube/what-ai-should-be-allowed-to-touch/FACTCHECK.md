## Claims asserted in narration, with article grounding

- Enslaved people sang "Satan, Your Kingdom Must Come Down"; couldn't own
  drums, gather freely, or write down meanings; sang about Satan's kingdom
  while meaning the masters' kingdom. Article opening. CONFIRMED (article's
  account).
- Blind Joe Taggart recorded it in 1931. CONFIRMED (article). Robert Plant
  sang it in 2010. CONFIRMED (article). African American Heritage Hymnal
  catalogued it as entry #485. CONFIRMED (article). Shirley Caesar sang it on
  Oprah's megachurch drama. CONFIRMED (article).
- Brown stripped every reference to Satan/Jesus/divine intervention and
  rewrote it as direct-address protest about earthly power ("Your kingdom
  must come down"). CONFIRMED (article).
- Production: five dollars, five hours; 1.5 million views in three months;
  99% approval on the remastered version. CONFIRMED (article's headline
  figures).
- Original future tense ("God will handle this") vs. Brown's present tense
  ("Heard it in the wind last night / Somethin' ain't sittin' right"). Article
  "What He Actually Did" section. CONFIRMED.
- Hymnal lyrics "The preachers are gonna preach your kingdom down / The
  deacons are gonna pray your kingdom down" replaced by "Built it high on
  broken backs / Sold the truth for paper stacks / But the people rise and
  the walls crack." CONFIRMED (article quotes both).
- Tactical ambiguity: Iranian protesters / Supreme Leader; Americans /
  oligarchy; "made it more portable." CONFIRMED (article).
- Traditional spirituals performed slowly ("funereal pace"); Brown's urgent,
  faster tempo, clearer beat, no haunting/moaning. CONFIRMED (article).
- Tech: Suno instrumental backing; "Mayfield King" vocal persona — an AI
  voice trained on his own speech patterns; Kling 2.1 for the video.
  CONFIRMED (article). Narration does not assert who the persona is named
  after beyond what the article states ("named for a Black soul legend" in
  the article's case-against section).
- Traditional costs: studio single $500–$2,000 minimum; professional music
  video $5,000–$50,000; weeks to months. CONFIRMED (article).
- The judgment passage: AI didn't decide which lines to keep, didn't
  recognize "Satan" was doing rhetorical work, didn't hear the phonetic
  lock of "broken backs"/"paper stacks". CONFIRMED (article's own wording).
- Case for: function preserved (code made explicit); "must" = natural law /
  prophetic register; explicit credits; "No Kings" framing as tool not
  authority. CONFIRMED (article's case-for section).
- Case against: theology as load-bearing structure; original's promise ("God
  will make this right") vs. replacement ("you're on your own"); the
  no-backlash observation. CONFIRMED (article's case-against section).
- The three explanations (don't understand / boundaries moving / function >
  form). CONFIRMED (article, numbered list; article says it believes #3
  while uncertain — narration matches).
- Numbers: original upload Oct 15 2025 — 448,529 views, 96%; December 6 2025
  remaster — 1,033,873 views, 99%; combined 1.48M in under three months.
  CONFIRMED (article). Narration says "about ninety days" — matches.
- Uncle Tupelo's version: "approximately 2 million Spotify streams
  accumulated over 33 years" — article's comparison. Narration says "roughly
  two million streams over thirty-three years." CONFIRMED (article's words).
  NOTE: article dates the Uncle Tupelo version to 1992; narration avoids the
  year, using only the 33-year span.
- The democratization frame: $50,000 → $5, months → hours; press/internet
  democratized distribution, AI democratizes creation; "the tools don't teach
  judgment. They execute whatever judgment you bring to them." CONFIRMED
  (article's phrasing).
- Lineage: "We Shall Overcome" (hymn → protest), "Bella Ciao" (rice fields →
  partisans → global anthem), "Lift Every Voice and Sing" (church → Civil
  Rights → climate activism). CONFIRMED (article).
- Closing: "The tools are neutral. The judgment isn't." — the article's
  conclusion, quoted. CONFIRMED.
