### CHAPTER 9: The Music Instinct — Evolution's Number One Hit
**Core Claim:** Music is an evolutionary adaptation, not a Spandrel. Evidence: ubiquity, antiquity (50,000-year bone flute), dedicated neural structures, cross-species analogues (bird song), genetic correlates (Williams syndrome). Proposed mechanisms include sexual selection (Miller), social bonding, cognitive development scaffolding, and mirror neurons as cultural transmission vectors.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Darwin's sexual selection hypothesis from *Descent of Man*
- Miller's argument: Jimi Hendrix's reproductive success as evidence for music-as-courtship-display
- Miller and Hazelton's menstrual cycle study: women at peak fertility preferred creative-but-poor men over wealthy-but-average men
- Blacking and Cross: ubiquity and inseparability of music/dance in non-Western societies
- Williams syndrome/autism double dissociation as evidence for a genetic cluster linking social and musical behaviors
- Rizzolatti's mirror neurons: firing during both action performance and action observation, found in Broca's area

**Logical Method:** Refutation of Pinker's Spandrel hypothesis + convergent multi-level evidence (evolutionary, neurological, cross-cultural, clinical).

**Logical Gaps:**
- The refutation of Pinker is partially accomplished. Pinker's claim that music "shows no signs of design for attaining a goal" is vulnerable to the antiquity/ubiquity arguments. But Pinker's deeper point—that music's evolutionary status is hard to establish because it could have piggybacked on language circuits while those circuits were being selected for—is not adequately addressed.
- Miller's sexual selection argument rests on Hendrix's reproductive success as evidence. This is a single high-profile case, selected because the person became famous. The vast majority of aspiring musicians do not achieve either fame or enhanced reproductive success. The survivorship bias is severe.
- The menstrual cycle study (Miller/Hazelton) is interesting but has a known replication crisis in this literature by 2010 onward. More importantly, it tests preference for creative vs. wealthy males—not musical males specifically. This is cited as evidence for music's sexual selection function, but the study is about creativity generally.
- The Williams syndrome/autism double dissociation is the chapter's strongest clinical argument, but the genetic link remains speculative ("some as yet unidentified genetic abnormality"). Levitin presents this as evidential support for a genetic cluster linking sociality and musicality, which is precisely what has not been established.
- Mirror neurons are invoked in the final pages as a mechanism for cultural transmission. In 2006, mirror neuron research was at its most optimistic phase; subsequent decades revealed significant problems with direct translation from macaque evidence to human language and music claims.

**Methodological Soundness:** The chapter successfully dismantles the most extreme version of the Spandrel hypothesis. It does not resolve the debate so much as establish that music cannot be *merely* auditory cheesecake. The positive case for which specific adaptive mechanism explains music remains unresolved and is honestly acknowledged as such by the chapter's end.

## Research Addendum (2026-06-03)

**Research date:** 2026-06-03

**Topic tags:** theory

**Research notes:**
- Music-theory claims should keep the elements distinct: pitch organizes high/low relations, rhythm organizes duration and pulse, melody orders pitches through time, harmony stacks or relates pitches, and timbre identifies sound color.
- For guitar/theory pieces, translate notation into action. Readers need to know what their fingers, ears, and attention should do differently after the concept is introduced.
- The strongest theory writing alternates between technical naming and audible consequence: what does the concept let the musician hear, predict, vary, or repair?
