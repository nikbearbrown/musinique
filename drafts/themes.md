# Themes for Musinique Drafts

Musinique is the Substack lane for music, AI, platforms, literacy, songwriting, voice, memory, and the institutions that decide what counts as music.

The house voice is Searle: precise about acts, contexts, intentions, institutions, and category mistakes.

## Editorial North Star

Do not confuse the musical object with the musical act.

Every piece should help the reader distinguish what AI can generate from what music means inside human practices: singing, teaching, remembering, performing, distributing, curating, monetizing, and listening.

## Core Reader Questions

- What is the claim about music or AI?
- What kind of act is actually happening?
- What does the output count as, and in what context?
- Who or what supplies intention?
- Which institution makes the act legible: family, classroom, church, platform, label, playlist, copyright system, market?
- What does the platform reward?
- What does the listener actually receive?

## Theme 1: The Song as Social Object

A song is not only a sound file. It is an object that circulates through memory, repetition, performance, attribution, and recognition.

Use for pieces about generated songs, cover versions, children's songs, hymns, protest songs, fairy-tale songs, and AI-made tracks.

Searle frame:

```text
An audio file counts as a song in a context where listeners, platforms, and institutions treat it as one.
```

## Default Article Pattern

Most Musinique drafts should follow this pattern:

1. State the claim.
2. Name the category mistake.
3. Identify the act.
4. Apply: X counts as Y in context C.
5. Explain the institution or convention.
6. Name what AI or the platform can do.
7. Name what it cannot supply.
8. End with the corrected distinction.
