### CHAPTER 7: Melody
**Core Claim:** Melody and vocals are the most important part of a song — approximately 70% of its power by the author's estimate. Melody should be simple, include rests, and be developed by humming over the chord progression. Lyrics follow from the melodic framework.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Author's personal estimate: 70% vocal, 30% harmony + rhythm ("my uneducated guess")
- Process: hum fragments, record immediately on mobile phone, loop to fix the line, repeat until complete
- Emphasizes rests as structural necessity: "A melody needs rests in order to have room to breathe"

**Logical Method:** Prescriptive sequence with explicit personal qualification of the key quantitative claim.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The 70/30 claim is the ebook's most significant structural assertion and its most honestly labeled conjecture. The author says "uneducated guess." This is commendable. But the claim functions as justification for spending proportionally more time on melody — and that prescription is not derived from the guess; it is merely adjacent to it.
- "Hum some notes over it" is the entire methodology for melodic invention. This is accurate — humming is how most non-theory guitarists develop melody — but it provides no guidance for a reader whose humming produces nothing useful. There is no scaffold for melodic failure.
- "Once you have a melody (or a fragment)" assumes you got one. The path for the reader who didn't is not addressed.
- The claim that knowing melody first determines "how many words and syllables you will need" is presented as a liberating constraint. This is a defensible framing, but it is also the point at which lyric writing becomes genuinely difficult — syllable count, stress alignment, vowel placement — and the ebook does not enter this territory at all.

**Methodological Soundness:** Honest about the primacy of melody; inadequate as a guide for developing one. The recording instruction is the chapter's most practically actionable advice.

## Research Addendum (2026-06-03)

**Research date:** 2026-06-03

**Topic tags:** theory

**Research notes:**
- Music-theory claims should keep the elements distinct: pitch organizes high/low relations, rhythm organizes duration and pulse, melody orders pitches through time, harmony stacks or relates pitches, and timbre identifies sound color.
- For guitar/theory pieces, translate notation into action. Readers need to know what their fingers, ears, and attention should do differently after the concept is introduced.
- The strongest theory writing alternates between technical naming and audible consequence: what does the concept let the musician hear, predict, vary, or repair?
