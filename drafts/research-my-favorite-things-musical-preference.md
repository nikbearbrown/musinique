### CHAPTER 8: My Favorite Things — Musical Preference
**Core Claim:** Musical preference is formed through prenatal exposure, early childhood acculturation, adolescent crystallization, and the inverted-U relationship between musical complexity and liking. The brain's reward system, cultural context, and personal safety (emotional vulnerability) all shape what music we will allow to move us.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Lamont's study: infants recognized music heard in the womb at one year of age
- Trehub: infants preferentially encode consonant intervals; the unequal spacing of Western scales confers processing advantages even to infants
- Saffran and Trainor on statistical learning of musical structure in infancy
- The inverted-U complexity/liking relationship (cited from aesthetic research across domains)
- Levitin's behavioral observation: musical tastes crystallize around age 18-20, parallel to myelination completion
- Schlaug: corpus callosum differences by age of musical training onset

**Logical Method:** Developmental research + psychoacoustic studies of infants + aesthetic preference theory.

**Logical Gaps:**
- Lamont's study is compelling but presents a methodological challenge Levitin doesn't address: mothers who played music during pregnancy may have continued playing it or humming it after birth, even if not the specific experimental piece. The womb exposure and post-birth incidental exposure cannot be cleanly separated.
- The "musical taste crystallizes by age 18-20" claim is presented as established finding ("several studies have found it") without citing those studies. This is a significant empirical assertion that would require peer-reviewed evidence.
- The emotional safety/vulnerability argument—that we allow music to move us when we trust the composer—is psychologically interesting but entirely unsubstantiated. No study is cited. This is Levitin's own introspective theorizing, which is valuable but should be marked as such.
- The inverted-U hypothesis for aesthetic preference is decades old (Berlyne) and has mixed replication. It also depends on measuring "complexity" relative to the listener, which is circular: something is too complex if you dislike it, you dislike it if it's too complex.

**Methodological Soundness:** The infant research is the chapter's strongest empirical foundation. The preference formation theory is plausible but leans heavily on unstated assumptions and unattributed claims.

## Research Addendum (2026-06-03)

**Research date:** 2026-06-03

**Topic tags:** cognition

**Research notes:**
- Music-cognition pieces should avoid reducing music to brain trivia. The interesting point is how perception, expectation, memory, movement, emotion, and social context make sound meaningful.
- For music-as-medicine or music-and-memory claims, keep the clinical language cautious: distinguish anecdotal therapeutic power from controlled evidence, and name the mechanism being proposed.
- The listener is not a passive receiver. Anticipation, repetition, surprise, entrainment, and memory help explain why a simple musical event can feel larger than its acoustic facts.
