### SECTION 4: Lesson 2 — Common Verse Types

**Core Claim:** Verses are constructed from a taxonomy of lyric types (abab/xaxa; aaaa/aabb) combined with specific harmonic and melodic development tools, and must contrast with the chorus to create effective section differentiation.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Two primary lyric form types are specified (Type 1: abab/xaxa; Type 2: aaaa/aabb)
- Development tools include: varied repetition, independent melodic ideas, counterpoint types, contrasting ideas, intensity-building melodic techniques
- Verse-chorus contrast is addressed in four dimensions: melodic, harmonic, melody/harmony relationship, and overall form

**Logical Method:** Parallel decomposition to Lesson 1—verse components are taught using the same categorical framework (harmony, melody, lyric, form), enabling comparison to the chorus framework.

**Logical Gaps:**
- The lyric form taxonomy (abab, aaaa, aabb) describes rhyme schemes, not lyrical content strategies. Cataloging rhyme patterns does not address why certain rhyme schemes pair more effectively with certain melodic shapes or emotional registers. The connection between lyric structure and musical structure is assumed, not demonstrated.
- "Chorus as the Emotional High Point" appears as a subheading in the verse lesson, suggesting this principle is being reinforced as a normative claim. But hit songs regularly violate this—verses carry emotional weight in singer-songwriter, folk, and certain R&B traditions. The normative claim is genre-dependent, not universal.
- "Top Line Writing" appears as a heading without context in this navigation document. Its placement in the verse/chorus contrast section suggests it addresses the relationship between harmonic support and melodic prominence, but the claim cannot be evaluated without content.

**Methodological Soundness:** The verse-chorus contrast framework is the course's most analytically substantive structural claim. Teaching contrast as a four-dimensional problem (melodic, harmonic, melody/harmony, formal) is genuinely useful decomposition. The categorical taxonomy appears sound for mainstream pop/rock/country applications.
