The Algorithm. I want to preface this section of the book by saying that I do not and I've never worked for Spotify. So all of this is based on my own research. I watched hours of content from Spotify developer conferences, independent individuals on YouTube, and read everything I could possibly find online regarding the Spotify algorithm. In addition to this, I tested my own theories based on this research with Facebook ad campaigns and Spotify playlist promotions to compare how songs performed with various types of promotion. More on that later. With that out of the way, here is the three tiered Spotify algorithm. First, collaborative filtering, second, web crawling, third, audio analysis. Collaborative filtering seems to be the largest and most significant portion of the Spotify algorithm. It sounds super fancy, but really it just means the Spotify collects a lot of data from users' behavior on the platform and correlates it in a meaningful way. For example, if 100 people all listen to the song A and save it to their library, and 10 of those people are also into song B and save it to their library. Spotify may recommend song B to the other 90 people who haven't heard of it yet. This process of collaborative filtering is extended throughout multiple areas of the platform, not just saving tracks to your library. It also applies to the artist's people follow the songs they listen to all the way through without skipping, the songs they add to their playlist, and more. Essentially, every single action you take on the platform is tracked and stored so that Spotify can correlate that data and make recommendations to people similar to you. Another common example is if that if your song appears between two particular artists and user playlists, your song will get recommended to other people who like those two artists but have never heard of you before. The next aspect of the algorithm is web crawling. You can think of this kind of like how Google search engine works. How does Google know what's on a website? It crawls the website to get pages on the site and stores portions of the content and keywords on the pages so that it can deliver great search results for its customers. Spotify does something similar to this for artists on the platform, meaning that it will crawl the internet looking for the artist and the releases by the artist to see what comes up. If you are often associated with certain other artists or covered frequently on certain blogs, Spotify will use this data to influence your related artists and potentially even algorithmic playlists. This particular avenue is a little bit of a black box, so treat this as more speculative than other aspects of the algorithm. Lastly we have audio analysis, which basically just means that Spotify analyzes the waveform of every song in the platform to determine its key, tempo, acoustickness, danceability, and other characteristics of the song. Spotify says that they use audio analysis to influence how songs are placed on the platform before they have data from collaborative filtering. Since collaborative filtering takes a lot of user engagement to make good decisions, if a large artist drops a song, they are able to use this audio analysis to put it onto playlists before they have the data available. In my opinion and experience this audio analysis does not seem to influence smaller artists, meaning that they just won't put you on a playlist and give you exposure, place base purely on audio analysis. It makes total sense why Spotify runs audio analysis on every song and uses that information to help decide who to recommend songs to. They can easily tell if your song is an upbeat dance track with vocals without a human needing to actually listen to it. A human may need to hear the song to find out if it's a good upbeat dance track with vocals, but they know where the song fits. There is a science to how DJs blend songs with different tempos and keys, and you can bet Spotify uses this information in its playlists. Remember, they are in the business of making the music listening experience as enjoyable as possible.

**Research notes:**
- Spotify-related claims should distinguish editorial pitching, algorithmic recommendation, playlist exposure, and paid/opt-in tools such as Discovery Mode. Treating them as one black box weakens the argument.
- Spotify defines artificial streaming around listening intent and manipulation, including bots/scripts and paid services that guarantee streams. For playlist-vetting pieces, the central test is whether listener behavior remains durable after exposure ends.
